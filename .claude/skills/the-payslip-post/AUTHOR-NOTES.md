# Author Notes: how this skill was built, so you can teach it (and build your own)

This is the behind-the-scenes doc. The skill itself is for the community member who wants to clone the post. This doc is for *you*, so you can teach the method, and so you can author your own skill from a post that worked for you.

If you're a community member reading this: the lesson here is bigger than one post. It's how to turn any proven thing into a tool you can run again and again.

---

## Where the stencil came from

This skill isn't a clever idea someone invented. It's one real post, reverse-engineered.

A UK financial planner sat down six weeks into LinkedIn, on an account with under 300 connections, and wrote one post about a pay rise that doesn't reach the bank. That post did 87,845 impressions and pulled 56 inbound connection requests from his exact ideal client. No CTA. A couple of weeks later he ran the same shape again and booked 10 calls.

Then the same bones got re-coloured for a $70M Australian property CEO, completely different country, tribe, everything, and did north of 175,000 impressions.

So before this was a skill, it was a thing that *actually pulled*, twice, for two tribes that have never heard of each other. That's the whole reason it's worth packaging. You don't build a skill around a theory. You build it around a result you can point at.

**The teach:** the strongest thing you can hand someone is proof, not advice. "Here's a shape that pulled 56 of his exact clients, watch it travel to a $70M bloke" beats any amount of "here's how to write a good hook."

---

## How the 7 bones were extracted

The post wasn't written to a formula. The formula was *found* in it, afterwards, by pulling it apart line by line and asking of each line: what is this doing, and why does it work?

That teardown surfaced seven distinct moves, each doing a specific job:
1. A logline hook that names the person and the painful number.
2. A now-moment that rides news the whole tribe is processing.
3. An object the tribe handles constantly, that their pain lives inside.
4. A deduction stack that says the unsayable truth in arithmetic.
5. A locked-away twist that closes the "but it's still mine" escape hatch.
6. An 10:32pm thought, the private thing named out loud.
7. No CTA.

The test that a bone is real: it survived the re-colour. When the same move showed up doing the same job in the property CEO's post and then again in the agency-CFO version, that's how you know it's a load-bearing bone and not a one-off flourish. A bone that only works for one tribe isn't a bone, it's just paint.

**The teach:** to extract a stencil from a post that worked, go line by line and name the *job* each line does, stripped of the specifics. The list of jobs is your skeleton. Then test it by trying to re-colour it for a different tribe. The moves that travel are the bones.

## Why it's an interview, not a template

This is the most important design decision, so here's the reasoning in full.

A template hands you blanks to fill in. The problem is the blanks come out sounding like everyone else who filled in the same blanks, because a template is a dead thing. Your tribe can smell a filled-in template, and the moment they do, the recognition breaks.

So this skill doesn't hand over a finished post. It makes the user do the colouring. It asks them, one question at a time, your tribe, your enemy, your object, your unsayable truth, your 10:32pm thought, and it refuses to skip the object step, because that's the bone the whole post hangs on. The post only comes out as *theirs* if they answered the questions and pulled the veto on the drafts. A workflow that spits out a finished post from a swipe is just handing them someone else's post with the serial numbers filed off.

And it's one question *at a time* on purpose. A wall of seven questions is a form, and forms get half-answered. One question, wait, react, next question, that's a conversation, and only a conversation gets the real, specific, usable answer. The whole reason it works is that it's a coach over the shoulder, not a fill-in-the-blank generator.

**The teach:** if you want output that sounds like the user, build the tool to *interview* the user, not to *template* them. Make them bring the colours. Protect the one step that matters most (here, the object) and refuse to let them skip it.

## How the references are bundled for distribution

This skill is a giveaway. It goes to community members who do *not* have access to the vault, the cookbook, or any of the source material. So everything it needs is bundled inside the folder, in plain English, with no links pointing anywhere external.

The structure:
- `SKILL.md`, the front door. The promise, the two rules, the seven bones summarised, and the interactive DECODE → RE-COLOUR → BUILD → CHECK flow.
- `references/the-stencil.md`, the core teaching doc. The seven bones in full, with the real example line beside the re-colour.
- `references/why-it-works.md`, the self-contained mechanics. So a cold user understands *why*, not just *what*.
- `references/find-your-object.md`, the deep dive on the hardest bone, worked across industries, with a strength checklist.
- `references/examples.md`, the same bones re-coloured for five tribes, so the user watches it travel.
- `references/voice.md`, keeping the output in the user's voice, not generic AI.
- `references/anti-ai-pass.md`, the de-AI checklist run on the output before showing the user.

The principle: a cold user with Claude plus this folder can run the whole thing end to end. Nothing assumes prior knowledge. Nothing points "back to the vault." Every mechanism it leans on is explained inside.

**The teach:** when you package a skill to give away, bundle everything. Assume the person has none of your context. The SKILL.md is the map; the references are the territory; nothing important lives outside the folder.

## A note on anonymity and honesty

Two things were handled carefully in the build, and they matter for anything you ship publicly:

1. **The planner stays anonymous.** His name and profession are kept off it. The point was never him, it's the bones. When you use a real client's win to teach, get their permission or anonymise them. A win you used to teach without asking is a fast way to lose the client.

2. **The object has to be honest.** The payslip image built for the property post stacked everything into one box, and the bottom line was deliberately labelled "in your pocket today," not "Net Pay", because no real payslip is laid out that way, and labelling it "Net Pay" would be a lie that the accountants in the comments would tear apart. Label the object as what it actually is and it's felt-true *and* literally true. That one line is the difference between a powerful object and a forgery. Same goes for every number: real, or it voids the post.

**The teach:** the recognition only works if it's honest. The second the right person catches you lying about the thing they handle every day, you lose them and the whole post with them.

## How YOU could author your own skill from a post that worked for you

This is the meta-lesson. You can do exactly what was done here, with a post of your own.

1. **Find a post that actually pulled, for a tribe, not a billboard.** One of yours, or one you can see real inbound happened on. Run the two tests: does the right person feel *seen* by it, and is that the person you sell to? If yes, you've got a stencil worth tracing.

2. **Decode it. Pull out the bones.** Go line by line. Name the *job* each line does, stripped of the specifics. That list is your skeleton.

3. **Test the bones by re-colouring once.** Take your skeleton and colour it for a different tribe. The moves that survive the swap are the real bones. The ones that don't were just paint.

4. **Write it up as a teaching doc + an interview.** A "the-stencil" file (the bones, with a worked example beside a re-colour). A "why-it-works" file (the mechanics in plain English). A "find-your-hardest-bone" file (the one step people get stuck on). An interview flow that asks for the colours one question at a time and refuses to skip the load-bearing step.

5. **Bundle it self-contained.** Plain English, no external links, everything inside the folder. So anyone with Claude and the folder can run it.

6. **Build in the guardrails.** The two rules that stop it being misused (here: tribe-not-billboard, and the object is non-negotiable). Without those, a skill drifts into producing slop.

That's it. Take a thing that worked, find the bones, build the interview, bundle it, ship it. The post you built it from is proof the campfire exists. The skill just lets someone else colour it in for their own.

Now go find a post that worked. Pull out its bones. And build the thing that lets a hundred people trace it.
