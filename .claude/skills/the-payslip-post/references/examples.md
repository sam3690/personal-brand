# Examples: the same 7 bones, re-coloured for different tribes

This is the proof the stencil travels. Below are the same seven bones, coloured in for five completely different tribes. Read them one after another and watch the skeleton stay identical while the paint changes entirely: the country, the object, the line items, the swear words, the exact 10:32pm thought.

The bones, every time:
1. Logline hook (the person + the painful number)
2. Now-moment
3. The object
4. Deduction stack
5. Locked-away twist
6. The 10:32pm thought
7. No CTA

---

## 1. The planner: high-earning specialists (the original)

*Tribe: the specialist profession who earns well, did everything right, and quietly keeps half of what they make. Anonymised.*

> For [these specialists], a £10,000 pay rise might only put £2,400 in your pocket.
>
> The pay review body just announced a 3.5% rise.
>
> Whether you think it's enough is one conversation. What actually reaches your bank account is another.
>
> Because you know how this plays out. Logging in. Downloading your payslip and scanning to the bottom right. Gross pay is up. But the net pay figure has barely moved.
>
> For people earning between £100,000 and £125,140, this is what a pay rise can look like:
>
> → 60% income tax (the tapering of the Personal Allowance)
> → 12.5% pension contribution
> → 2% National Insurance
> → and sometimes 9% student loan
>
> That's up to 76p deducted from every extra pound earned in that band. On a £10,000 rise, that can leave £2,400 reaching your bank account.
>
> The pension contribution is real money, but it's not money you can touch until retirement. And even then, a penalty for taking it early.
>
> So the pay rise lands. Tax takes most of it. The pension locks the rest away.
>
> And now you're back, doing the extra hours, missing your kids' sports game, wondering what you're even taking home for all of it.

**Object:** the payslip, scanned to the bottom-right. **Result:** 87,845 impressions, 56 inbound from his exact client, off a 300-connection account six weeks old. Then 10 booked calls running the same bones a fortnight later.

## 2. The property CEO: high-income Australians who feel punished for it

*Tribe: the $250k PAYG Aussie who works their guts out, pays full tax, and feels the system rewards the truly rich and punishes the responsible. Re-coloured from the planner's post.*

> $250k PAYG is now a poor Aussie battler.
>
> A $10,000 pay rise clears about 4 grand.
>
> And in 5 days it gets worse. July 1, Division 296 switches on. They get their hand into your super.
>
> They call it the fair go.
>
> Thursday, you open Employment Hero, scroll past the gross to the net, then check it against what hit the CommBank account. Gross jumped ten grand. The bank moved by bugger all.
>
> Because on every extra dollar over $190k:
>
> → 47c gone the second it's earned. Top marginal + Medicare levy.
> → Let your private cover lapse, the Levy Surcharge bolts on another 1.5%.
> → Crack $250k and Division 293 rips 15% out of your concessional super.
> → HECS still on the books? Indexed every June.
>
> You're keeping less than half of what you flogged your guts out for.
>
> And the super they took? Locked till you're 60. Now Division 296 stacks another 15% on the earnings of anything over $3m. They didn't index the three mill either.
>
> All while you're flogging yourself at work and missing the young lads' Saturday footy game.

**Object:** Employment Hero payslip + CommBank balance. **Result:** north of 175,000 impressions, 1,300+ reactions. Same bones as the planner's quiet payslip post, Aussie paint.

*(Note: every number in a post like this has to be verified against the live tax rules before posting. A made-up figure gets torn apart in the comments and voids the whole thing.)*

## 3. The fractional CFO: agency founders who are the poorest person in their own company

*Tribe: the founder doing $2M who, on payday, pays every single person in the building before themselves and takes home less than their own mid-level employee.*

> You did $2M this year and took home less than your account manager.
>
> It's awards season, so the feed's full of "we hit $2M ARR" wins.
>
> Here's the post nobody puts up.
>
> The morning after payroll runs, you open the business account. And you look at the number that's left once everyone else has been paid.
>
> That number is the truth. The $2M is the headline.
>
> Run the maths on a payday:
>
> → wages out the door first, every time
> → super on top of every wage
> → PAYG you're holding for the tax office, not for you
> → the software subscriptions nobody remembers signing up for
> → the office no-one's used since Tuesday
>
> You get paid last, if there's anything left. Some months there isn't.
>
> And the "profit" on the P&L? Half of it's trapped in work-in-progress and 60-day debtors. Money you can't touch, can't pay yourself with, can't take to the supermarket.
>
> So the revenue grows. Everyone gets paid. The profit sits in someone else's overdue invoice.
>
> And you're back at your desk at 7pm, looking at that bank balance, wondering whether you built a company or just bought yourself the worst-paid job in it.

**Object:** the business bank balance the morning after payroll. No tax, no politics, proves the bones travel anywhere.

## 4. The divorce lawyer: the newly-single parent rebuilding (a fresh one)

*Tribe: the recently-separated parent, often the one who left or got left, now running a household solo on one income in a house built for two. Built fresh on the same bones.*

> You kept the house in the settlement. It's now the most expensive thing you own and can't afford.
>
> School's gone back this week, so the costs all land at once.
>
> Everyone told you "fight for the house." Nobody did this maths with you.
>
> Sunday night, you sit at the kitchen table with the bank app open and you do the sum you've done forty times. One income now. Same mortgage. Same rates. Same everything that used to split two ways.
>
> → the mortgage that was comfortable on two salaries
> → childcare and before-school care, because there's no second adult to do the run
> → the half of "shared costs" that's quietly become all of it
> → the legal bills still landing months after it's "done"
>
> And the spare room. The one that's an office now. You walk past it every morning and it still feels like the room it used to be.
>
> The settlement's signed. The house is "yours." And it turns out yours means you carry the whole thing on half of what carried it before.
>
> And it's 10:32pm and you're still up, not because of the paperwork, but because you're working out whether you can keep the one thing you fought hardest to keep.

**Object:** the kitchen-table bank app + the spare room that's now an office. Notice: still a thing, still a ritual, still a moment. Zero numbers that need fact-checking, because the line items are universal household costs, not tax rules.

## 5. The bookkeeper: tradies drowning in their own admin (a fresh one)

*Tribe: the sole-trader tradie, sparky, chippy, plumber, brilliant on the tools, terrified of the paperwork, who shoves every receipt in the ute and dreads BAS time. Built fresh on the same bones.*

> You turned over $300k on the tools and you've got no idea if you actually made money.
>
> BAS is due in a couple of weeks, so the shoebox is staring at you.
>
> Here's how it goes, and you know it goes like this.
>
> Sunday arvo, you tip the ute receipts out on the kitchen table. Fuel dockets, hardware slips, that invoice you wrote on the back of a smoko bag. And you try to remember which job each one was for.
>
> → the GST you've been spending all quarter that was never yours
> → the super you keep meaning to sort and haven't
> → the invoices you sent and never chased, sitting unpaid for ninety days
> → the cash jobs you can't quite work out how to put through
>
> You're flat out booked for the next three months and you still couldn't tell me your actual profit.
>
> And the money that looks like it's in the account? Half of it's the tax office's. You just haven't moved it yet. Spend it now and you'll be short come BAS.
>
> So the work rolls in. The shoebox fills up. The number you owe creeps up where you can't see it.
>
> And you're lying in bed Sunday night, knackered, doing a job you're good at, with no clue whether you're actually getting ahead or just busy.

**Object:** the shoebox of receipts in the ute, tipped out on the kitchen table. Trade tribe, blue-collar argot, same seven bones exactly.

## What to notice

Read all five back to back and watch what stayed and what changed.

**Stayed (every time):** the person-plus-number hook, the now-moment, the object you point at instead of describing, the deduction stack in arithmetic, the locked-away twist that closes the escape hatch, the named 10:32pm thought, the deliberate absence of a CTA.

**Changed (every time):** the country, the income level, the object, the line items, the brand names, the swear words, the exact private thought.

Same skeleton. Five completely different bodies. That's the whole promise. The shape is universal. The paint is yours.
