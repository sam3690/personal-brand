# Post 3 — "The grounding gap"

---

Two AI agents did the same job. One was right 94-99% of the time. The other was right 10-31% of the time.
Same task. One design choice.

The difference is called grounding, and it decides whether an AI agent is safe to put in front of your client's customers.

An ungrounded agent answers from its training data. It sounds confident either way, whether it's right or making something up. That's where the 10-31% accuracy numbers come from, and it's also why "the AI said something weird to a customer" stories keep happening.

A grounded agent can only answer from what you actually gave it: an approved script, a real CRM field, a document you wrote. If the answer isn't in there, it says so instead of guessing. Grounded, context-aware systems are landing 94-99% accuracy in current benchmarks, and RAG-grounded customer-facing agents show 60-80% fewer hallucinations than the ungrounded version of the same task.

The plain version, for anyone who isn't building these: the difference between a bot that embarrasses your client and one that just works is whether it's allowed to make things up. Grounding is the setting that turns that off.

The objection I hear most is "my client won't want a bot talking to their customers." Fair. My answer: you approve every word before it ever ships, and a grounded agent literally cannot say anything you didn't write or approve. It's not improvising. It's retrieving.

Have you actually tested what your AI tool says when it doesn't know the answer, or are you assuming it says "I don't know"?
