A vendor you depend on will get breached eventually. n8n just did.
What decides whether that breach ever reaches your client's system isn't the vendor. It's five checks most builders skip.

I'm not writing this to pile on n8n. I build in it every day and I'll keep building in it.

I'm writing it because "the vendor got breached" and "my client's data leaked" are two different events, and the gap between them is entirely on whoever built the system in between. That's me. Here's exactly what I check, in order:

1. Scope every credential to one workflow. Not one login shared across ten builds. If workflow A leaks, workflow B stays untouched.
2. Kill unused connections. Every third-party tool with standing access is one more door into the system. Connected something once to test it? Disconnect it when done.
3. Rotate on a calendar, not a memory. The accounts that get hurt worst in a breach are usually the ones that hadn't rotated anything in months. A recurring reminder costs nothing.
4. Separate environments. The workflow touching a client's real data is never the same instance used to test a new node.
5. Read the vendor's incident report when it drops, and actually check whether I did 1 through 4, not just whether the vendor patched their side.

Run all five and a vendor breach becomes their bad week, not your client's data leaving the building. Skip them and it's the same weekend either way, you just find out later.

This is the same discipline behind every system I build. It has to survive a bad day, not just a demo.
