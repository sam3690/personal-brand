n8n, the tool I build every client's lead-response system in, got breached this month.
136 records exposed. 5 with password hashes.

A zero-day in Metabase, a BI tool n8n used internally, let someone in without logging in.

n8n found out August 6. Published a full incident report. Shipped a patch August 20.

Most people read a line like that, decide it's not their breach, and scroll past.

Here's what I actually took from it, four years into building backend systems for healthcare orgs, where a leak isn't an inconvenience, it's a patient record:

The tool getting breached was never the risk I was managing.
The risk I was managing is what happens to a client's system if MY access gets breached.

Scoped credentials, one per workflow, never a master key shared across builds.
No standing connection to a third-party tool I'm not actively using.
Rotation on a calendar, not "whenever I remember."

n8n did the right things after the fact: disclosed fast, patched fast, notified the right people. That's the bar to clear, not the failure.

The lever was never picking a tool that never gets breached. Every tool eventually does.
It's building so the breach stops at my front door, before it ever reaches a client's.

That's why my builds start boring on purpose, security first, before anything else.

What's the last vendor security notice you actually read past the headline?
