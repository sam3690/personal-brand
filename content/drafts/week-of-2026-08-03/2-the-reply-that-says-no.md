You raised your minimum to $15k. Then you answered a $6k powder room at 9pm, because saying no felt rude.

The words you typed into the Instagram DM were "we could probably work something out."

I do not think you have an inquiry problem. I think you have a saying-no problem.

Same week, the cabinet shop quoted a vanity top it did not want, and the remodeler drove out to a bathroom he already knew he would not bid. Different number, same reason.

Most of what lands in that inbox is not your project. Answer all of it fast and all you have done is fill next week with drives you did not need to make.

Three questions before you get in the car, then the exact no and the exact yes, word for word. Eight slides.

Every first message I have written to a studio owner this year has been ignored. So I have thought hard about what makes a stranger answer a message, and what makes her delete it.

Send me your minimum and the two kinds of project you will not take on, and I will send back the no message written for your studio. If you want it in your own voice rather than mine, paste in three sentences you have actually written to a client and I will match it. Ten minutes, and it is yours whether or not we ever speak again.

One argument while you are here. Does your minimum belong on your website, or only in the first reply? I think the first reply and not the site, and I know half of you will disagree.

**FIRST COMMENT:** The obvious objection: what if the $6k powder room turns into the whole first floor in two years?

Sometimes it does. The referral version of the no covers that better than a reluctant yes ever will. She got two real names from you, the small project went well, and you are the person who was straight with her instead of the one who resented her budget for four months.

That is a better phone call in 2028 than a powder room you took in 2026.
