I went through the front doors of the design studios on my Austin list, the way a client would. Every one of them had at least three.

The contact form. The number on the Google listing. The email at the bottom of the page.

The fourth one is Instagram, and if that is where your portfolio lives, that is where your inquiries live too.

I could not find one of the nine where an inquiry lands in a person's inbox. hello@. info@. interiors@. Nobody's name on any of them, and a Saturday form sits in there until Monday.

I could be wrong about all of it. I have not seen one of their inboxes, only what a client sees standing outside.

None of the four tells the other three that somebody walked in. The only place they meet is your thumb, at 9pm on a Sunday.

A homeowner cannot tell from a portfolio what you are like to work with for fourteen months. A door nobody answers does not read as a busy studio. It reads as the studio that will be hard to reach for the next fourteen months.

If you run the cabinet shop, your doors are the showroom phone, the website, the email on the side of the truck, and the designer who texts you direct. Same problem. None of them tells the other three.

The last system I built was for an interior design studio, one person running it. Inquiries sat about 24 hours because the owner was on site. Now it answers in seconds, on WhatsApp, in the studio's own words. Never short of inquiries. Short of answered ones. The owner's words: "big difference, saving me so much time."

Something answers the door at 9pm, sounds like you, and puts the real ones on your calendar. The rest it turns down politely, so you stop having the coffee you did not want to have.

If you want, DM me your studio or shop name and I will walk your own doors on a Saturday night, the way a client would, then send you three minutes of video on which door loses the inquiry. It stays between us, and you do not owe me a call for it.

Which door do your best projects actually come through? I would guess Instagram, and I would like to know where I am wrong.

**FIRST COMMENT:** The number I would want if I ran a studio is not how many inquiries came in. It is how many got answered the same day. The one studio I asked had never counted, because nothing they owned counted it.
