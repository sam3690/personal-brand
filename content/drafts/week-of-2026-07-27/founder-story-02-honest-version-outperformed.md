# Founder story 2: "100 impressions on my best work. 1,500 on the post where I admitted I have not made it yet."

**Sequel to `founder-story-built-couldnt-sell.md`** (posted Tue 2026-07-28), which broke every reach
record on the account: 1,500 impressions, 1,056 members reached, 18 reactions, 5 comments, 12 profile
viewers, 3 followers, 95% out-of-network. Prior best in the performance log was 196 impressions.

Angle: the gap between those two numbers IS the insight. Chapter two reports the result of the honest
post honestly, finds the same mistake in how he opens sales conversations, and names the belief it left
behind. Readers who engaged with chapter one get a real payoff instead of a repeat.

Level 1 (hero) throughout. **No Level 3 turn, no guide layer, no method disclosure** (see v3). The job
of this post is recognition and a thought worth reading on a Sunday morning, nothing else.

## v4 changes (Usama, 2026-08-01) — the axis of the comparison

Usama's note: *"this was not best vs worst post, this was showcasing my work vs people getting to know
my side of the story, so the hook should be like that."*

Correct, and it was a real error in v1 through v3. The post kept measuring one of his posts against
another on **quality** ("my best work", "the post I put the least expertise into", and the media hook
"MY WORST POST DID 15X MY BEST ONE"). Two problems with that axis:

1. **It is not what happened.** Both posts were good. One showed his work, one told his story. The
   audience did not prefer a better post, it responded to a different kind of thing.
2. **It quietly runs down the work he sells.** Calling the frameworks and cost breakdowns his worst
   post invites a reader to agree. He sells that work. It is not the loser in this comparison, it is
   simply the other category.

Changed on this axis:
- Opening: "100 impressions on my best work / 1,500 on the post where I admitted I have not made it
  yet" → **"100 impressions when I showed my work / 1,500 when I told my side of the story."**
- "The post I put the least expertise into travelled the furthest" → **"The post that showed none of
  my work went the furthest by a distance."**
- Media hook rebuilt on work-versus-story, with the best/worst framings explicitly rejected in the brief.

**Standing rule:** in this register, never rank his own posts against each other. Contrast the
*category* (work shown vs story told), never the *quality*.

## v3 changes (Usama, 2026-08-01) — READ THIS FIRST, it defines the register

Usama's note: *"you are revealing my strategies here, I just want to share my insights and influential
words that people would love to read as morning motivation, not the approach I will use from now on."*

He is right and this is now a standing rule for founder-story posts. v2 cut the beats that made him
look commercially weak, but it still ended on his operating manual. Two things were wrong with that:

1. **It gave away positioning for free.** "The order changes: the real number before the impressive
   one. Where I actually am before what I can do." That is how he intends to sell, published as a
   memo. Anyone can lift it, and prospects who read it later recognise the technique being run on them.
2. **It is the wrong genre.** A method announcement asks the reader to watch him improve. An insight
   hands the reader something they can use on their own Sunday. Only the second one gets shared, and
   sharing is the whole reason the 07-28 post reached 1,056 people.

**Cut in v3:**
- "For months I opened every conversation the same way I wrote those posts. With the system..." (his
  sales approach, disclosed)
- "The conversations that finally moved were the ones where I said none of it, asked what a missed
  enquiry actually costs them, and then stopped talking." (his actual discovery technique, disclosed)
- "So the order changes: the real number before the impressive one. Where I actually am before what I
  can do." (his playbook, disclosed)

**Replaced with** a universal observation carrying no method at all: everyone leads with a version of
themselves that runs ahead of where they are, and nobody recognises themselves in a finished thing.
The reader gets the insight. Nobody gets the playbook.

**Standing rule going forward:** in founder-story posts, report what happened and what it means about
people. Never publish what Usama will do differently in his own sales or content. The result is his to
share. The method is inventory.

## v2 changes (superseded, kept for the record)

1. **Cut the cold-email beat entirely.** v1 said "I went back and read the first 16 cold emails I sent
   this month. Zero replies." Usama's call, and it is correct: he sells marketing automation and
   outreach systems. A prospect who reads that his own outreach gets no replies has no reason to buy
   an outreach system from him. The insight was right, the surface was wrong.
   **Replaced with the sales-conversation beat**, which carries the identical lesson (leading with the
   build instead of the cost of the problem) but reads as sales craft he has already fixed rather than
   a broken funnel. Sourced from chapter one: "Things only moved when I stopped describing what I could
   build and started asking people what their problem was actually costing them."
2. **Rhythm pass.** Broke the flat "Frameworks, cost breakdowns, tool stacks" list into single-word
   sentences. Moved the 95% out-of-network stat to its own line as the sharpest number in the post.
   Replaced the announced turn ("Here is the part that actually bothered me") with a flat statement
   ("I do not think that is a lesson about posting") so the pivot is earned, not signposted.
3. **Cut "It is Sunday, so I will leave it there."** It was a device. The reflective register carries
   the Sunday feel without labelling it, and the post now ends on the belief straight into the question,
   which is a harder close.
4. **"Hardest one to relate to" → "hardest one to trust."** Trust is the actual currency of this
   framework and the sharper word.

## Post

100 impressions when I showed my work.
1,500 when I told my side of the story.

For three months I posted the things I am actually good at. Frameworks. Cost breakdowns. Tool stacks. Careful work. Most of it reached about 100 people and stopped there.

Last Tuesday I posted the honest version instead. That I still work 9 to 6. That I have built 500+ workflows and earned $850. That I am not where I said I would be by now.

1,056 people reached. 18 reactions. 5 comments. 12 profile clicks and 3 new followers.

95% of that reach came from people who do not follow me.

The post that showed none of my work went the furthest by a distance.

I have been sitting with that all week.

Most of us are carrying a version of ourselves that runs a few years ahead of where we actually are. We lead with that one. It is cleaner. It has no bad months in it. It is the version we would rather be judged on.

Nobody has ever seen themselves in a finished thing.

People see themselves in the middle of something. The part that is still going. The part you would rather not put a number on yet.

And almost all of us are in the middle of something this morning.

The thing you are most tempted to leave out is usually the only part somebody else needed to hear.

What is the true sentence about your work that you keep editing out?

# Media brief

**Decision: lead with the raw analytics screenshot. Usama's own photo second, or not at all.**
See the reasoning note below. Never composite the two into one designed card.

- **Type:** Multi-image post, 2 images. Image 1 does all the work.
- **Image 1 (the proof):** the unedited LinkedIn analytics panel from the 2026-07-28 post. Do not crop
  it into a pretty frame, do not add a border, do not add brand colours. The whole point is that it is
  a receipt, and receipts are not designed. Include the discovery block so the 1,500 impressions, the
  1,056 members reached and the 5% / 95% network split are all readable, plus the profile activity
  block underneath. If the screenshot is low resolution, retake it rather than upscaling.
- **Image 2 (the person, optional):** a plain photo of Usama. Not a brand card, not a quote overlay,
  no text on it at all. It exists so the 95% of readers who do not follow him can attach the story to
  a face and click through. If the only available photo is a polished promotional headshot, leave it
  out; a formal headshot next to a "here is my unfinished receipt" post fights the post.
- **Text on image (hook overlay, goes on the PHOTO, never on the screenshot):**
  `I SHOWED MY WORK: 100 PEOPLE.` / `I TOLD MY STORY: 1,500.`
  Anton uppercase, Bone White, "1,500" in Signal Green. Two lines, nothing else on the card.
  Rationale: the contrast is **work versus story**, not best versus worst. It is not a ranking of his
  posts, it is two different things a person can put in front of an audience. It states a contradiction
  the image cannot resolve, so the only way to close the gap is to open the post. If the hook card
  exists, it leads and the screenshot follows as the proof.
  Approved alternates, all on the same axis: `NOBODY CAME FOR THE WORK. 1,500 CAME FOR THE STORY.`
  (sharpest, single line) · `THREE MONTHS OF MY WORK: 100 PEOPLE.` / `ONE POST ABOUT ME: 1,500.` ·
  `THEY DID NOT WANT THE FRAMEWORKS.` / `THEY WANTED THE PART I LEFT OUT.` (most curiosity, least
  numeric) · `95% OF THESE PEOPLE DO NOT FOLLOW ME` (if the network split is the visible focus).
  **Rejected framing, do not reuse:** anything built on best post / worst post ("MY WORST POST DID 15X
  MY BEST ONE"). It reads as a quality ranking of his own output, which is both wrong and slightly
  self-deprecating about work he is selling. The frameworks and cost breakdowns are not his worst work.
  They are his work. The other post was his story. That is the whole distinction.
  **Never overlay text on the analytics screenshot.** Its whole value is being an unretouched receipt,
  and typography on it makes it read as produced, which is the one thing this post argues against.
  Fallback if both images are already final: use the primary hook as the first line of the first comment.
- **See-more hook (post body, do not change):** mobile truncates around 140 characters, which lands
  just after "1,500 on the post where I admitted I have not made it yet." The number contrast is fully
  visible above the fold and the resolution is not. That is the correct break point.
- **Alt text, image 1:** "LinkedIn analytics for a post from 28 July 2026 showing 1,500 impressions,
  1,056 members reached, 5% in-network and 95% out-of-network, with 12 profile viewers and 3 followers
  gained from the post."
- **Alt text, image 2:** "Usama Ayoub."
- **Do not build:** the designed two-number comparison card that was specced in v1. It was a good card
  and it is the wrong instinct for this post. A designed graphic about how polish underperforms is the
  exact contradiction the post is written against.

## Reasoning: screenshot only, or screenshot plus his photo?

**Recommendation: post both, screenshot first. If you only want one, use the screenshot.**

For the screenshot alone: the entire post rests on numbers the reader cannot verify, and the screenshot
is the verification. It is the single highest-trust asset available for this post. It also earns dwell,
because people stop and read a real analytics panel in a way they do not stop for a designed card, and
dwell is what pushed the 07-28 post out-of-network in the first place.

For adding his photo: 95% of the last post's reach was people who do not follow him, and only 12 of
them clicked the profile. Those readers met a story with no face attached to it. A plain photo as
image 2 gives chapter two a person to remember, which is exactly what this framework is for
(recognition, not reach). Chapter one had no face and still worked, so this is an upgrade, not a fix.

Why the screenshot must be first: LinkedIn renders image 1 largest and it is what appears in the feed
preview. The receipt is the hook. His face is the follow-through.

The one thing to avoid: pasting his photo onto the screenshot, or wrapping either in a branded frame.
That turns the receipt into an ad, and the post is an argument against exactly that move.
