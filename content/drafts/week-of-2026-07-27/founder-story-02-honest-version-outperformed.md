# Founder story 2: "100 impressions on my best work. 1,500 on the post where I admitted I have not made it yet."

**Sequel to `founder-story-built-couldnt-sell.md`** (posted Tue 2026-07-28), which broke every reach
record on the account: 1,500 impressions, 1,056 members reached, 18 reactions, 5 comments, 12 profile
viewers, 3 followers, 95% out-of-network. Prior best in the performance log was 196 impressions.

Angle: the gap between those two numbers IS the insight. Chapter two reports the result of the honest
post honestly, finds the same mistake in how he opens sales conversations, and names the belief it left
behind. Readers who engaged with chapter one get a real payoff instead of a repeat.

Level 1 (hero) for the first half, then a Level 3 turn: names the hole in the "build authority by
teaching value" advice everyone on this platform follows. No guide layer, no client named, no case study.

## v2 changes (Usama, 2026-08-01)

1. **Cut the cold-email beat entirely.** v1 said "I went back and read the first 16 cold emails I sent
   this month. Zero replies." Usama's call, and it is correct: he sells marketing automation and
   outreach systems. A prospect who reads that his own outreach gets no replies has no reason to buy
   an outreach system from him. The insight was right, the surface was wrong.
   **Replaced with the sales-conversation beat**, which carries the identical lesson (leading with the
   build instead of the cost of the problem) but reads as sales craft he has already fixed rather than
   a broken funnel. Sourced from chapter one: "Things only moved when I stopped describing what I could
   build and started asking people what their problem was actually costing them."
2. **Rhythm pass.** Broke the flat "Frameworks, cost breakdowns, tool stacks" list into single-word
   sentences. Moved the 95% out-of-network stat to its own line as the sharpest number in the post.
   Replaced the announced turn ("Here is the part that actually bothered me") with a flat statement
   ("I do not think that is a lesson about posting") so the pivot is earned, not signposted.
3. **Cut "It is Sunday, so I will leave it there."** It was a device. The reflective register carries
   the Sunday feel without labelling it, and the post now ends on the belief straight into the question,
   which is a harder close.
4. **"Hardest one to relate to" → "hardest one to trust."** Trust is the actual currency of this
   framework and the sharper word.

## Post

100 impressions on my best work.
1,500 on the post where I admitted I have not made it yet.

For three months I posted the things I am actually good at. Frameworks. Cost breakdowns. Tool stacks. Careful work. Most of it reached about 100 people and stopped there.

Last Tuesday I posted the honest version instead. That I still work 9 to 6. That I have built 500+ workflows and earned $850. That I am not where I said I would be by now.

1,056 people reached. 18 reactions. 5 comments. 12 profile clicks and 3 new followers.

95% of that reach came from people who do not follow me.

The post I put the least expertise into travelled the furthest by a distance.

I do not think that is a lesson about posting.

For months I opened every conversation the same way I wrote those posts. With the system. The build, the nodes, the reliability, the four years of backend work sitting behind it. All true. All impressive. All of it useless to the person listening.

The conversations that finally moved were the ones where I said none of it, asked what a missed enquiry actually costs them, and then stopped talking.

Same mistake in both places. I was leading with the finished version of me. The version with no bad months in it.

That version is the hardest one to trust. It is also the one that is not true yet.

So the order changes: the real number before the impressive one. Where I actually am before what I can do.

What is the true sentence about your work that you keep editing out?

See the reasoning note below. Never composite the two into one designed card.

## Reasoning: screenshot only, or screenshot plus his photo?

**Recommendation: post both, screenshot first. If you only want one, use the screenshot.**

For the screenshot alone: the entire post rests on numbers the reader cannot verify, and the screenshot
is the verification. It is the single highest-trust asset available for this post. It also earns dwell,
because people stop and read a real analytics panel in a way they do not stop for a designed card, and
dwell is what pushed the 07-28 post out-of-network in the first place.

For adding his photo: 95% of the last post's reach was people who do not follow him, and only 12 of
them clicked the profile. Those readers met a story with no face attached to it. A plain photo as
image 2 gives chapter two a person to remember, which is exactly what this framework is for
(recognition, not reach). Chapter one had no face and still worked, so this is an upgrade, not a fix.

Why the screenshot must be first: LinkedIn renders image 1 largest and it is what appears in the feed
preview. The receipt is the hook. His face is the follow-through.

The one thing to avoid: pasting his photo onto the screenshot, or wrapping either in a branded frame.
That turns the receipt into an ad, and the post is an argument against exactly that move.
