# Website Wedge: Instagram DM scripts (opened 2026-08-17)

For the Instagram-only local food businesses (brownie/cake seller, chocolate seller) and anyone
found the same way. Governed by WEBSITE WEDGE in `../strategy/current-strategy.md`: paid except the
one UF Designs build, same template not bespoke, and the site is the door while the order flow is
the product. Register follows `inbound-dm-playbook.md` rule zero: **mirror their message length.**

## Why DM beats email here
These people live in their DMs, because DMs are their order channel. A cold email to a home baker
goes unread; a DM gets opened within the hour. That is the whole reason this lane exists as DMs.
The flip side: they open it expecting a customer, so be clear about who you are in line one. A DM
that pretends to be an order and turns into a pitch burns the account.

## The sequence

| Step | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Get their handle, screenshot 6-10 of their product photos and their price list | before anything |
| 1 | Fork the template into THEIR brand: name, photos, flavours, real prices, their WhatsApp number | ~1 hour |
| 2 | Host it on a subdomain, e.g. `theirname.yourdomain.com`. Live link, not a file | 10 min |
| 3 | Send message 1 with the link | late morning or after 8pm, not mid-bake |
| 4 | One bump if silent after 3 days. Then let it die | day 3 |
| 5 | Price only when they ask. Half upfront before any further work | on reply |
| 6 | Order-capture automation sold separately, ~30 days after the site is live | later |

**Never send the `Batch` demo.** The entire power of the open is that they are looking at their own
shop. A generic demo makes it a portfolio link, which is what everyone else sends.

## Message 1 (the opener)

> Hi [name], I've been seeing your [brownies] on my feed for a while.
>
> I build sites for small food brands, and I wanted to see what yours would look like as one, so I
> made a page for [brand] over the weekend: [link]
>
> Open it on your phone. Tap a few [flavours] and hit the button at the bottom, it writes the whole
> order into WhatsApp for you.
>
> Nothing owed either way. Worth finishing?

Why it is shaped this way:
- Line 1 is a specific, real observation. Not "I love your page."
- Line 2 states who you are before any ask. No bait.
- Line 3 tells them the one thing to do. Most people will not find the order builder on their own,
  and the order builder is what sells the site.
- "Nothing owed either way" kills the what-is-the-catch reflex before it forms.
- One question. Low cost to answer, and yes is the easy answer.

**Do not** attach a portfolio, a PDF, a price, a call ask, or the word "free" in message 1.

## Message 2 (one bump, day 3, then stop)

> No worries if it's not for you, just wanted to check the link actually opened. Some phones block
> it: [link]

Gives them an easy out and a reason to reply that is not "are you buying." One only. Chasing a
local SMB in DMs costs the account.

## Branches

### "How much?" — the most likely reply
> Rs 35,000 for the page. Half to start, half when it goes live.
>
> That covers the design, your photos in it, the WhatsApp ordering, and getting it on a domain.
> I'd need your photos and your current prices from you. Live in about a week.

Number first, no hedge, no menu. Then what it includes, then the next step. Never "it depends."

### "That's too much"
> Fair. There is a shorter version: one screen, your flavours, the same WhatsApp ordering, without
> the scrolling. Rs 22,000, same delivery.

**Cut scope, never cut the number.** That is what `simple.html` is for. It is a rung, not a
discount, and it must never be described as one. If they balk again, let it go.

### "Can you also do my Instagram posts?"
> Yes, but let's do the site first and see if it earns its keep. One thing at a time.

Do not stack a second service onto an unsigned first one.

### "Let me think about it"
> Sure. The page stays up a couple of weeks if you want to show it to anyone.

Then stop. No third message.

### "Can you do it free / for exposure"
> Can't do that one. The build has real hours in it and I'd rather do it properly and stand behind
> it. If Rs 35,000 is not there right now, the shorter version at Rs 22,000 does the same job.

Refuse on your own cost, never on their worth. Same construction as the LinkedIn playbook.

## Price notes
Starting ask **Rs 35,000** for the deluxe page, **Rs 22,000** for the simple one. Floor is
Rs 22,000; below that this is not worth the hours and it teaches the referral network a bad number.
**50% upfront before any further work, no exceptions** — this is the UF Designs lesson and it is
not reopened for a smaller client.

Also part of the deal, stated before you start, not after:
- Their photos and prices provided by them
- A credit line in the footer, and permission to show the site in your portfolio
- A written line of feedback once it is live

Never quote these numbers in the same conversation, thread or document as the $500/$1,500
automation pricing. Different product, different buyer, different anchor.

## The upsell (about 30 days after the site is live, not before)

> How are the orders through the site been coming in?
>
> If any of them land when you can't answer, I can set it up so every one gets a reply inside a
> minute even while you're in the kitchen, and nothing sits unread. Separate piece of work, tell me
> if it's worth talking about.

This is the actual business. The site is the door. Do not open with this, and do not bundle it into
the first yes: it doubles the price and halves the close rate on a buyer who has not bought anything
from you yet.

## Do not
- Do not offer a meeting as the ask. The link does the meeting's job. Meet only if they ask.
- Do not mention n8n, automations, agents, or AI in message 1.
- Do not send a voice note first. Text plus link, so they can tap it.
- Do not build a second free site. One free build exists and it is UF Designs.

**Price:** ask **600 AZN**, floor **400 AZN**. Roughly $350 / $235, deliberately above the Karachi

**The proof line if they hesitate:** a taplink is a list of links. It cannot take an order, and it
cannot present a xonça, which is the most expensive thing they sell.

4. Send Brownies by T. Wait a day. Whatever you learn from that reply, apply it, then send choco_nua.

## PRICING CORRECTION (2026-08-18, Usama's call)

The Rs 35,000 / Rs 22,000 ladder above was set before the build existed and it is wrong on two counts.

**1. It bought the wrong comparison set.** Rs 35,000 for a custom-coded site with a scroll-driven
sequence and a working order builder is student-with-a-template money in Karachi. This is the exact
error already documented in `../strategy/current-strategy.md` PRICING FRAME about the $500 number:
pricing at the subcontractor rate does not read as good value, it reads as an inexperienced supplier,
and it selects for the buyer who wanted cheap.

**2. It bundled a recurring cost into a one-time fee.** Domain plus hosting plus a year of "my site is
down" messages is an ongoing obligation. Sold inside a fixed price it produces zero recurring revenue,
an awkward year-two conversation, and an unpaid support relationship attached to Usama's own server.

**Corrected ladder:**
| | Build (one time) | Keep it live (yearly) |
|---|---|---|
| Animated build | **Rs 60,000** | Rs 12,000/yr, first year included |
| Simple one-screen build | **Rs 35,000** | Rs 12,000/yr, first year included |

Half upfront, half when live. Never meet in the middle: **cut scope, not the number.** The old ask
becomes the new floor, which is the correct shape for the ladder.

**Why the yearly fee matters beyond this deal:** this business has zero recurring revenue anywhere in
it. A hosting-and-updates fee is the cheapest recurring line it will ever build, it is normal and
expected for websites, and every site sold from here compounds it. Do not discount it away to close a
build. It is worth more over two years than the discount saves.
