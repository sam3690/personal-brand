# Inbound DM Playbook (LinkedIn)

For when a stranger DMs asking to "connect" or "collaborate." Most of these are peers with
no budget. The job is to find out which in one message, without burning the relationship.

## Rule zero: mirror their message length

If they write one-liners, reply in one or two lines with exactly ONE question. A long reply
to a short message reads as "waiting to be asked," which signals pitch, not peer. The offer
explanation and the call ask belong in exchange two or three, after they answer.

## The opening reply

One question, no menu:

> Depends what collaborate means on your side, running the outbound or trading notes?
> I build outbound systems so either works.

That question is the whole filter. Someone looking for free labor has no good answer to it.

## Branch on the answer

| They say | Do |
|---|---|
| "Run it for me, we split revenue / equity" | Decline the split, counter with a founding slot at $500. |
| "I'd pay, what does it cost" | Real prospect. Book the call. |
| "Just trade notes / co-market" | Cheap yes. 20 min, zero build. |
| "Let's collaborate now, sort money later" | Decline on cost grounds, counter with the founding slot. |
| Vague / dodges | Let it die. One follow-up max. |

**Time cap: one call.** Do not build a list, write sequences, or do a "quick audit" as a
favor. That is the trap in every one of these conversations.

## Ready messages

### "Collaborate now, figure out money later"

> Straight answer: I can't do that one, and not for the reason you'd think.
>
> Outbound has real upfront cost. Domains, inboxes, warmup, verified data. That comes out of
> my pocket on day one and it doesn't care whether we figure the money out in month three.
> Every "sort it later" deal I've seen ends with one person having spent money and the other
> having spent goodwill.
>
> What I can do is make the entry small enough that it isn't a real risk for you. [founding slot].
> If it books you nothing, you've spent less than a month of your own tool bill and you still
> own the setup.
>
> If that's genuinely not there right now, no hard feelings, ping me when you've got your
> first paying customers and we'll do it properly.

Refuse on YOUR cost, not their worth. Keeps it non-insulting. The number being small turns
"I can't" into "I won't," which tells you everything.

### The pilot

**The $300 cold-email-infrastructure pilot is dead as of 2026-08-09 (CEO ruling after the market
research).** Three reasons: it is a different product from the lead-response build and needs separate
delivery; it sits below every reference price found (a live n8n job ad pays contract engineers
$500-$1,200 per build, and competitors' *pilots* run $1,500); and it was pointed at pre-revenue peers
who cannot pay. Do not offer it. Do not revive it without a CEO decision.

**What to offer instead when someone wants a smaller first step:** the founding slot on the real offer.
$500 for the first 2, then $1,500, and frame it as what it is, two founding slots bought in exchange for
a named reference and a testimonial with a number in it. Never as "I'm cheaper."

That framing is not cosmetic. Discounting does not buy past the trust gap: Upwork's own commissioned
economist report finds US freelancers earn 82% more from the same clients and are 51% more likely to be
hired after controlling for the rate bid, and the NBER paper behind it measures the substitution
elasticity at 0.039. Buyers who filter on country leave rather than trade down. A lower price only
reduces what you collect from the buyers who were going to hire you anyway.

Before saying yes, ask: what's your price point, and have you closed one customer yet, even
manually? If no, do not sell. Tell them to close three by hand first. Losing the deal to gain
someone who says you were the one who did not sell them something is a good trade.

### Equity / rev-share ask

> No equity or rev share pre-revenue, on either side. Rev share only makes sense when there's
> a proven close rate to share, and neither of us has that yet. Flat fee, small, keeps it clean.

### Lowering the barrier without conceding the sale

Ends the message, only if they framed it peer-to-peer:

> And if it turns out you just want to compare notes, that works too. I'd rather have one
> honest conversation with someone building than ten polite ones. No pitch either way.

"No pitch either way" is load-bearing. Never say "even if you don't buy," that pre-concedes.

## The 20-minute call

**0-5, they talk.** What's the product, what do you charge, have you closed a single customer
any channel, who exactly is the buyer, what have you tried and what happened.

**5-10, diagnose out loud.** Deliver as questions, not verdicts. Three stacked criticisms is
an attack and they stop buying.

- Not "your ICP is too wide" but "is the buyer the owner of a 5-man crew or an ops manager at
  a 60-person shop?" They hear the problem themselves.
- Not "you have no proof" but "have you closed one yet, even a friend?" If no: "then touch 1
  can't reference results, it has to lead with their pain. Copy constraint, not a dealbreaker."
- Market facts can be stated flatly, they're not about the person. Example: general contractors
  live on job sites, not in inboxes, so email read rates run at the low end.

**10-15, the approach at the level of shape.** Targeting logic, the 3-touch structure and why
day 0/3/7, domain separation, realistic reply rates. Concepts, not artifacts.

Domain separation, stated correctly: the brand domain is an asset, the sending domain is a
consumable. The main domain (website, invoices, real client mail) never touches cold outbound,
ever, because its reputation cannot be rebought. Buy a separate lookalike sending domain, warm
it 2-3 weeks, send everything cold from there. If it burns, throw it away and buy another for
$12. The secondary is the permanent outbound rail, not a stopgap.

**15-20, land it.** Either the pilot, or the honest "you're not ready, go close three by hand."

## The free / paid line

Diagnosis is free, deliverable is paid. Consultants give away the what and charge for the done.

| Free on the call | Costs money |
|---|---|
| "Your targeting is too broad, split by size" | The actual filtered contact list |
| "Touch 1 should lead with their pain, not features" | The written sequence |
| "Don't send from your main domain" | Domain bought, inboxes set up, warmed |
| "GCs answer phones, not email" | Any of it executed |

## Landmines

- **The proof boomerang.** The moment you say "you have no proof," the next question is what
  results you've gotten. Have the honest version ready. Right now: machine built, in-market,
  small sample, no conversions yet. Don't volunteer it, don't bluff. If you bluff and they dig,
  you lose the deal and the peer relationship.
- **No invented stats.** "Single digit reply rates are normal for cold B2B" is true and safe. A
  specific percentage you made up is a trap if they've read anything.
- **Don't chase.** If they go quiet after a message that already offered the call, do not send
  "tell me when you're in for the call." That's two in a row from you and it assumes a yes they
  never gave. React to one of their posts instead, then one low-pressure follow-up after 3-4
  days, then stop.
