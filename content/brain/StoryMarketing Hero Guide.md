# StoryMarketing Hero Guide

Source: Benjamin Chan, "The Death Of Traditional Personal Branding" (2026-03-12), captured 2026-07-28.

**The problem:** value + time + first-mover = authority. Two of the three are gone for us. Titans hold
~95% of attention through **accumulated recognition** and raw volume never catches them. The only
variable you can accelerate is recognition itself, and storytelling is the accelerator, because a story
makes the reader live it instead of hearing about it.

**The 5-Year Test (a hook worth stealing):** "If you continue at your current strategy and rate of
growth, will you actually be where you want to be in 5 years?"

**Story spine:** hero wants something → hits a problem they can't solve alone → at their lowest point a
guide appears with a plan → they win. That's every story ever.

**The core move: be BOTH.**
- Hero only = entertainment, nobody buys.
- Guide only = one of a thousand helpful experts, invisible.
- **Authority converts, interest attracts.** You need both.

**3 Levels**
1. **Storytelling** (you = hero): who are you, what do you want, what's at stake. Chan's 2nd-most-viewed
   post had nothing to do with his offer and still brought in clients.
2. **Storymarketing** (you = guide): who you help, how, and their real pains + objections. A cold reader
   must be able to answer these by scrolling your feed. Use ICP-only vocabulary.
3. **Storybranding**: find the **hole** in the market (what the gurus preach that doesn't work), call it
   out, become "the guy". See [[One-Sentence Positioning]].

**How we use it:** ~1 founder/hero post per 4-5 posts, as the trust layer on top of the default
[[Winning Post Patterns]] register. Founder-story posts carry **no offer and no CTA keyword**, same
exception as [[Payslip Post Skill]]. Stakes must be specific and true: vague hardship reads manufactured
and burns the trust the format exists to build.
