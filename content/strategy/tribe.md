# Tribe: The Founder-Designer

> **Owner:** CEO agent. Derived from `content/strategy/current-strategy.md` (the SSOT). If the niche in
> current-strategy.md changes, this file is void and must be rebuilt before any agent writes from it.
> **Created:** 2026-08-03 · **Rev 2 (post-critique rewrite):** 2026-08-03 · **Niche window:** 2026-07-24 to 2026-10-22
> **Supersedes** the generic candidate answers in `content/brain/Usama Tribe Worksheet.md` for all
> interior-niche content. Reading order: `current-strategy.md` → this file → `winning-post-patterns.md`.
> **Voice:** no em dashes anywhere, including these headers. **US English only.** Every line a claim or a proof.

**What this file is for.** Matt Lakajev's test: a niche is a spreadsheet row, a tribe is who someone
already is. "Interior designers" is a row. This document turns it into a tribe you can write to, so the
right person reads a post and says "holy shit, that is me" instead of "nice tip". Nothing here gets
invented at write time. Agents pull from this file.

**What changed in rev 2.** Two hostile critics, one of them role-playing an actual studio owner, killed
five things in rev 1: the British spelling, the flagship proof number, the central causal claim, the
invented night scene, and two fabricated hooks. All five are fixed below. The corrections are marked
**[REV 2]** so no agent reintroduces the old version from memory.

**Pronoun note:** the studio founder in this tribe is usually the principal designer and more often a
woman than a man. Default to "she" or "you". Never write "small business owners". This is a writing
default, not a stat: **never put a gender ratio in a post**, there is no sourced number on file.

## 0. The one line

**The owner-operator of a design, remodel, or kitchen-and-bath studio, 1 to 6 people, who put her own
name on the door, is booked through spring, quietly scared about summer, and keeps finding out weeks
later that she was never one of the two studios the client called back.**

## 1. THE CHANNEL RULING (CEO, binding, read before anything else)

The growth critic is right on the facts: a residential interior designer opens LinkedIn maybe once a
month, and every documented Lakajev win is a LinkedIn-native tribe. The answer is not to abandon a niche
the CEO committed to for 90 days. The answer is to split **subject** from **reader**.

**1. The emotional SUBJECT of every post stays the founder-designer / studio owner.** She is who the
stories are about, whose objects get named, whose night gets described. She is the person the offer
serves. Recognition comes from her, always.

**2. The READER OF RECORD on LinkedIn is the LinkedIn-present part of the same niche.** All of these are
already named in-niche by `current-strategy.md`:
- design-build and remodel firms
- kitchen and bath dealers
- custom cabinetry and millwork shops
- showroom and multi-line rep groups
- the operations manager or studio manager inside a larger design firm

These people open LinkedIn weekly, they stand next to the designer on every project, and they have the
identical lead-response wound at larger deal sizes.

**3. Channel roles, fixed:**
| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| Cold email (batch-04 method) | DISCOVERY for the solo designer |
| Instagram DM | DISCOVERY for the solo designer |
| LinkedIn feed | (a) the credibility surface a prospect checks after a cold email or DM lands, (b) organic reach to the LinkedIn-present adjacents above |
| LinkedIn connection request plus DM | the only LinkedIn lane that survives a buyer who opens the app monthly. The inbox waits. The feed does not. |

**4. The two-reader rule for every draft.** A post that only a solo residential designer could recognize
is **half a post**. The best post is recognizable to the designer AND legible to the remodeler, the
cabinet shop, and the kitchen-and-bath dealer standing next to her. Write the specific designer detail
first, then check that the adjacent operator reads it and thinks "that is my Tuesday too."

**5. No reach-based publishing block.** Nothing in this file may be used to hold a post because a
previous post underperformed, or because no in-niche commenter appeared. The account's number one
measured leak is over-generating and under-shipping. Reach findings append to
`content/performance/linkedin-performance-log.md`. They never stop the line.

## 2. THE THREE BOXES

### 2.1 CHOSEN TRIBE: you run your own studio

**Definition. [REV 2, widened back to the SSOT]** The **owner-operator of a design, remodel, or
kitchen-and-bath studio, roughly 1 to 6 people**. Not "works in design". Chose to own it. She left a
firm, an architecture practice, a showroom, a builder, or a corporate job, and took the risk of putting
her name on the invoice. Home staging is in. Design-build is in. Landscape design is in. Custom cabinetry
and millwork are in. The tribe is not the profession, it is the ownership.

**The initiation (what it actually cost to get in). [REV 2]**
Not a credential. Residential does not require NCIDQ in most states, and many of the best studio owners
never sat it, including second-career designers with a waiting list. The real dues, every one of which
every member has paid:
1. Getting the resale certificate and the sales tax permit.
2. The first trade account application, and waiting to be approved by a stranger.
3. Eating the first custom mistake out of her own pocket: the sectional in the wrong performance velvet,
   22 weeks out, no returns.
4. Floating client money on a PO, and learning what it feels like when the client is late on a draw.
5. Carrying an employee's salary through a month with no installs.
6. Filing the LLC and signing the first LOA she wrote herself.

(NCIDQ, ASID, IIDA, NKBA and CKBD exist, and licensure matters in the states that require it. Mention
them as furniture, never as the price of entry. A post that gatekeeps on a credential writes out the
self-taught half of the tribe, which is the half most likely to buy.)

**Shared suffering (the price of membership).**
The client who fires you over a paint color. The husband who "saw it and hates it" after the presentation
was already approved. The GC who framed the niche four inches off and wants a change order to fix his own
error. The marble that arrives chipped and the freight claim nobody wants to own. The install day where
everything lands except the rug. The discontinued fabric that dies between the presentation and the PO.

**Recognition markers (a real studio owner clocks each of these in one second).**
1. Has a resale certificate and knows exactly which vendors ask for it before they will quote net.
2. Has trade accounts: Kravet, Schumacher, Visual Comfort, Four Hands, Made Goods, Arteriors, Loloi.
3. Has an LOA and a retainer, because she once worked three weeks without one.
4. Has a stated project minimum, and has broken it at least twice in the last year.
5. Bills some mix of design fee plus product markup, and has changed that model at least once.
6. Owns a fan deck and has an opinion about White Dove versus Alabaster versus Swiss Coffee.
7. Has taped a sectional footprint on a bare subfloor so a client could stand inside it.
8. Goes to High Point in April and October and comes home with 40 memos and a sore back.
9. Knows what "the acknowledgment says 22 weeks" does to a schedule she already promised out loud.
10. Runs on Studio Designer, Ivy inside Houzz Pro, DesignFiles, Mydoma, Programa, or a spreadsheet she is
    ashamed of.
11. Has driven to the slab yard to tag a specific piece of quartzite, personally.
12. Has a workroom, an upholsterer, a stone fabricator and a millworker she trusts, and would rather lose
    a job than lose them.
13. Has been asked to "just send over a few paint colors" as a favor.
14. Has a punch list open right now on a project that was supposed to be done.
15. Has a shoot scheduled, or is waiting on the photographer, or is chasing a magazine that has not replied.

**Adjacent markers (the LinkedIn reader of record, per §1).** Carries a showroom or a shop floor. Quotes
off someone else's drawings and eats the takeoff error. Has a lead time board on the wall. Waits on a
designer's finish selections to release a job. Loses a $60k cabinetry package because the homeowner "went
another way" and never says why. Same wound, bigger number, and they are on LinkedIn on a Tuesday.

**Their vocabulary (write these words, not marketing words).**
FF&E · spec, spec'd, spec sheet · to the trade, trade only, designer net, net versus MSRP · resale cert ·
LOA, retainer, scope creep, change order, out of scope · PO, acknowledgment, ack, ETA, lead time, "22 weeks
out", backordered, "it's on the water" · memo, pull memos, CFA, COM, yardage, repeat, railroaded, double
rubs, Wyzenbeek, performance fabric, Crypton, Sunbrella, bouclé · the workroom, the upholsterer, the
fabricator, the millworker, the trades, the GC · receiver, receiving warehouse, white glove, blanket wrap,
concealed damage, freight claim · site visit, walkthrough, punch, punch list · install day, reveal, styling
day, the shoot · the boards, concept board, elevations, renderings, SketchUp, Chief Architect · full
service, e-design, one-room refresh, paid consult, discovery call, "our minimum is" · slab yard, template,
honed, leathered, waterfall edge, inset versus full overlay · Sub-Zero, Wolf, Thermador, the appliance
package · High Point, Vegas Market, KBIS, the design center, D&D, theMART, DCOTA, the Dallas Design
District · ASID, IIDA, NKBA, CKBD · Business of Home, "A Well-Designed Business" · Luxe, Veranda, Milieu, AD.

**Banned vocabulary when writing to her.** leads, funnel, pipeline, speed-to-lead, response time,
conversion, nurture, supplies, stone supplier, "small business owner", "local service business". Say
inquiry, or say people. Say the slab yard. Say "she answered that night".

**Three sanctioned exceptions, and only these three.**
1. Hook 13, where "the lead that pays for it" is the punchline and the studio owner who read it said keep it.
2. Hook 5, where the word is being **quoted off her own Meta dashboard**. The platform's UI says "leads", so
   repeating it is her reality, not our vocabulary. The rule bans us calling her clients leads, not us
   reading her screen back to her.
3. The offer name itself, **"The 7-Day Lead Rescue"**, which is set in `current-strategy.md` and cannot be
   changed by this file. **Open item for the CEO:** the offer name trips our own vocabulary rule every time
   it is spoken to this tribe. "The 7-Day Inquiry Rescue" says the same thing in her words. Recommend the
   rename at the next weekly review. Until then, prefer describing the offer over naming it.

**Objects they can picture instantly.** The 25 ft tape in the door pocket. The Benjamin Moore fan deck.
The tote of memos rubber-banded by project in the back of the SUV. The High Point badge still on the
lanyard hanging off the mirror. The iPad with the presentation on it. The painter's tape. The touch-up kit
and the steamer that live in the car from October onward.

**Taboos.** Never reveal net cost. Never let the client order it themselves off Wayfair. Never let the GC
pick finishes. Never start without a signed LOA. Never work for exposure. Never say the word "cheap".

**Status markers.** Getting published. Being invited to a showhouse. Raising the minimum and having it
stick. Booking a year out. A studio address instead of the dining table. A project manager hire. A product
line or a collab. Being the designer other designers refer overflow to.

### 2.2 PLACE TRIBE: where she actually is

**Definition.** Not one city. Two overlapping places: a US metro with real renovation money, and
Instagram, which is where her portfolio, her competition and her inquiries all live at once. She is
physically in the first and commercially in the second.

**Austin, Texas is the live place. [REV 2]** The entire current prospect list is Austin: 9 interior design
and home-staging studios sourced from Google Maps, ratings 4.7 to 5.0, review counts 6 to 63. Several are
effectively solo. Two of the nine are home staging. Write Austin first, and treat Dallas, Nashville,
Scottsdale, Charleston, Naples, Denver and Atlanta as the next metros, not the current one.

**Recognition markers.**
1. Works a metro with a luxury pocket, and says the neighborhood, not the city: Westlake, Tarrytown,
   Rollingwood, Barton Creek, Clarksville in Austin. Preston Hollow, Green Hills, Paradise Valley,
   Old Naples, Buckhead, Sullivan's Island elsewhere.
2. Drives between three jobsites and a showroom in one afternoon and eats lunch in the car.
3. Instagram is the portfolio. The website is a formality her clients check after Instagram.
4. Pinterest is where the client's expectations were formed, before she ever met them.
5. Houzz Pro sends her leads and an invoice, and she is not sure the two are related.
6. Google Business Profile with 4.7 to 5.0 stars and somewhere between 6 and 60 reviews. Every review is
   about taste, communication or timeline. Nothing else.
7. Inquiries arrive in four separate places that do not talk to each other: Instagram DM, the website
   contact form, a call to the mobile number, and email.
8. Local Facebook groups, an ASID or IIDA or NKBA chapter, and a group chat with three other designers
   where they vent about the same GC.
9. Twice a year the whole tribe is in the same building: High Point, April and October.
10. She is on LinkedIn maybe once a month, and it is not where she gets work. **Write that down, then read §1.**
11. Client communication is text-shaped, not email-shaped: a photo at 9:47pm captioned "quick question".
12. The vendor rep, the GC and the workroom are all in separate threads on separate apps.

**The trade-adjacent places (where she is standing when she is not at her desk). [REV 2]**
The local design center or the drive to the Dallas Design District. The showroom floor, and the rep who
knows her by name. The GC's job site at 7:30am before the crew starts. The slab yard. The receiver's
warehouse. The Instagram grid, which is not social media to her, it is the portfolio. The client's group
text, where she is one of five people and the only one who is being paid. High Point in April and October.

**Their vocabulary for this place.** "The DMs" · "the algorithm" · "reels" · "saves" · "she found me on
Instagram" · "Houzz lead" · "the contact form" · "my Google reviews" · "market" (meaning High Point, never
a marketing term) · "the showroom" · "the design district" · "she's in Westlake" · "the referral came from
my GC".

**Objects.** The phone with 400 jobsite photos and a red badge on Instagram. The Houzz Pro "you have a new
lead" email. The Google review notification. The saved-posts folder full of other designers' work. (The
Meta ads dashboard is a real object but a minority one: most studios this size either never ran ads or got
talked into them and turned them off. When a single paid object is needed, **Houzz Pro is the safer one**,
because far more of them are paying for it and quietly resenting it.)

**Place accuracy rule.** Never write a place detail you cannot verify. A wrong neighborhood, a design
center that does not exist in her city, or a showroom she has never heard of is the same credibility tell
as a British spelling. If unsure, say "the showroom" and move on.

### 2.3 CIRCUMSTANTIAL TRIBE: the part she did not choose

**Definition.** She trained to have an eye. Nobody trained her to be the salesperson. The studio has grown
past the point where she can personally answer every inquiry, and has not grown far enough to hire someone
whose only job is answering them. That gap is the circumstance. 1 to 6 people, and it is where the offer
lives.

Layered on top: the market cooled from the 2021 peak. In 2021 she turned work away. In 2026 the phone is
quieter, and that is exactly when she started spending money to get it ringing again. (Do not attach a
multiplier to this. "Worth five times what it was" is invented and a real operator will price it instantly.)

**The split that changes the pitch. [REV 2]**
| Sub-type | Who touches the inbox | The actual pain | The pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo (1 to 2)** | Her, between jobsites | Nobody answered | "It answers while you are on site, in your words." |
| **Has an assistant (3 to 6)** | A design assistant or studio manager | Somebody answered **badly**: 14 hours late, or opening with "what is your budget?" | "It answers the same way every time, and it says no politely so your assistant never has to." |

Roughly half the live Austin list is the solo case. Write to solo by default, and make the second column
legible so the four-person studio does not feel described as a hobby.

**Recognition markers.**
1. She is the designer, the salesperson, the project manager and the accounts-payable department, and
   three of those four happen after 6pm.
2. Second career is common: teacher, retail buyer, marketing, real estate, or the parent who redid a
   friend's house and never stopped. (Writing rule only. Never put an age bracket, a family situation, or
   a demographic descriptor in a post. That reads like a data broker describing her.)
3. Booked solid through spring and holding a hole in the calendar four months out that she has told
   nobody about.
4. Just hired the first employee, or is one month from having to, and is doing the payroll math at night.
5. Raised the minimum project fee, then answered a project below it anyway.
6. Cannot answer the phone between 9am and 5pm on install week, which is the exact week the inquiries land.
7. Has lost a job and only found out why weeks later, secondhand, from a friend of the client.
8. Believes the story is "not enough leads", and has never counted how many inquiries she got versus how
   many she answered.
9. Says yes to a coffee, a walkthrough and a "quick look" for free, because turning it down feels rude.
10. Has a Notes app list of names to call back with no phone numbers next to them.
11. Sells six to eighteen months ahead of install without ever having said that out loud.

**Their vocabulary for this circumstance.** "I'm slammed" · "I'm wearing all the hats" · "it's feast or
famine" · "I'm booked through March but June is empty" · "she went dark" · "they ghosted me" · "I need to
follow up with her, I keep meaning to" · "I'm not a salesperson, I'm a designer" · "my leads are terrible"
· "I don't want to sound desperate" · "I'll get to it tonight".

**Objects.** The unanswered DM. The Sunday quoting session. The proposal tab still open from Thursday.
The unread badge that does not go down.

## 3. THE MERGED TRIBE (the trilogy)

**The paragraph.**

You left a firm and put your own name on the door. You have a resale certificate, a Kravet account, a fan
deck in the door pocket and a tote of memos in the back seat that has not been sorted since April. You are
in Austin, on a jobsite three days a week, and your portfolio lives on Instagram because that is where
your clients actually look. You are the designer, the salesperson, the PM, and the person who chases a
22-week acknowledgment, and nobody ever taught you the second half of that list. The last two years got
quieter, so the inquiries that do come in matter more than they used to, and they land at 9pm on a Sunday
from people who are comparing three studios in one evening. You are booked through March and quietly
scared about June. And the last project you lost, you did not lose on taste. You never got to taste. She
called two studios back and you were not one of them, and you found out in March, from her sister.

**The one sentence.**

The owner-operator of a 1-to-6-person design, remodel or kitchen-and-bath studio in a US metro, booked
through spring and scared about summer, who keeps getting cut before the interview while she is standing
at the slab yard.

**Bio-line versions (profile, banner, DM openers, cold email subject). [REV 2]**
- "I answer the inquiries you miss while you are on a jobsite."
- "Every inquiry answered in under 60 seconds, wherever it comes in: Instagram DM, form, text, missed call."
- "For the designer who is also the salesperson."
- Banned: "speed-to-lead" (agency language, the exact phrase in the DMs she already deletes) and any
  plural that implies a book of clients ("studios I work with"). Singular only: "the last one was for an
  interior studio."

**The anti-version, by POSTURE not by headcount or price. [REV 2]**
- **The inbox already has a paid owner.** A firm with a front desk, or an agency already answering for
  them. No pain, no sale.
- **The volume-and-template posture.** The $99-a-room e-design shop and anyone selling a look rather than
  a project. A lost inquiry is worth $99. The math does not hurt.
- **The pure lead-gen buyer.** Wants more leads, measures cost per lead, will not read a qualifier, and
  will resent a system that says no on their behalf. Wrong posture, will churn.
- **The pure price-bid trade.** Roofing, gutters, a GC who only bids plans someone else drew. They compete
  on the number, not on taste. Different enemy, different post. (A design-build remodeler who sells the
  design is **in**, not out.)
- **Agency owners, founders, AI builders.** The old ICP. Writing to them is the single most likely failure
  mode of every agent in this system. See §8 for what the cold email data does and does not prove.
- **"Small business owners", "entrepreneurs", "local service businesses".** Not tribes. Spreadsheet rows.

**Deleted from rev 1:** the hard $10k average-project floor and the blanket design-build exclusion. Both
contradicted the SSOT and disqualified at least 2 of the 9 live Austin prospects (both home staging).
Deal size is a signal, not a gate.

Exclusion is still the point. A post all of the above could nod at is a billboard.

## 4. THE RECOGNITION TEST

Ten statements only this tribe nods at. Hook feedstock. Each is tagged for whether the LinkedIn-present
adjacent (§1) can read it too.

| # | Statement | Adjacent reads it? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You have spec'd an entire living room around a fabric that got discontinued between the presentation and the PO. | Partly (the shop knows discontinued) |
| 2 | You have taped a sectional out in painter's tape on a bare subfloor so a client could stand inside it. | Yes, every remodeler has watched this |
| 3 | You know exactly what "the acknowledgment says 22 weeks" does to a timeline you already said out loud. | **Yes, hard** |
| 4 | Your best inquiry this month came in at 4:40pm on a Friday while you were at the slab yard. | **Yes** |
| 5 | You have a resale certificate, a Kravet account, and no idea who answered your DMs last Thursday. | Partly |
| 6 | You drove to High Point, came home with 40 memos, and eleven unanswered inquiries. | Yes (swap High Point for KBIS) |
| 7 | Someone has asked you to "just send over a few paint colors", and meant it as a favor to you. | Yes (the shop version: "just ballpark it") |
| 8 | You raised your minimum to $15k and then answered a $6k inquiry at 9pm because it felt rude not to. | **Yes** |
| 9 | You have re-read a Saturday contact form on Monday morning and known, before you replied, that it was already gone. | **Yes** |
| 10 | You found out in March, from her sister, that she signed with someone else in January. | **Yes** |

**Test for any new statement:** could a marketing consultant, a roofer or an AI builder write it? If yes,
it fails. Could a designer have said it to another designer at a showroom without explaining a single
word? If yes, it passes. **Then the §1 second test:** would the cabinet shop owner reading over her
shoulder recognize his own week in it?

## 5. THE ENEMY

Framed how she would frame it, out loud, to another designer. Not how a marketer would frame it.

**The surface enemy:**
- "People treat design like a commodity now."
- "Pinterest gave everyone $200k taste and a $30k budget."
- "They're shopping three studios in one night."
- "The $99 e-design people are training clients to think this is cheap."

**The real enemy. [REV 2, the whole document turns on this]**

**Your reply time is being read as your project management.**

Rev 1 said speed wins the job. It does not, and a real designer will screenshot that claim and send it to
her group chat as a joke. Nobody hands a $40,000 job to whoever texts back first. She interviews two or
three studios. The client came from a GC or from a friend's house. They read the portfolio for three
weeks. They choose on taste, and on whether they can stand you for fourteen months.

Here is the true mechanism, and it is worse:

1. A client cannot judge taste. Not really. Not before the first meeting.
2. So they judge the proxies. Reply time is the loudest proxy available, and it is the only one they can
   read on a Saturday at 9pm from their phone.
3. **Speed does not win the job. It decides whether you are one of the two she calls back.**
4. A slow reply is not read as "she is busy and in demand". It is read as "this is how she will run my
   project", which for a fourteen-month renovation is the exact thing the client is most afraid of.

That is not an injustice. It is how anyone buys a fourteen-month relationship. Framing it as an injustice
sounds like flattery. Framing it as a mechanism sounds like someone who has run a business.

**The stance, unchanged and load-bearing.** Usama is not against her. He is against the axis. The system
does not make her a better designer. It stops the wrong axis from deciding who gets interviewed. **She is
not the problem. She is on a jobsite.** Every post about speed carries this or it reads as a scold.

**What the enemy is NOT (do not write these):** "her mindset", "she's disorganized", "she doesn't have a
CRM", "she needs a funnel". Every one of those makes her the problem, and a post that implies she is slow
or disorganized loses her permanently.

## 6. THE NIGHT THOUGHT

**[REV 2] Rev 1 put a lost-lead monologue at 10:32pm with a lamp on and a laptop half closed. That scene
is deleted. It is a stock photo and it was written from outside the window looking in.**

**What is actually happening at 10:32pm.** She is doing the thing she could not do at 2pm: cutting a PO,
chasing a freight claim on chipped marble, redoing an elevation, or answering the 9:47pm "quick question"
text with a photo attached. And underneath all of it, the thought is cash:

> "Did the draw hit. I owe the workroom Friday and the client has not wired. I am carrying a junior's
> salary through a month with no installs."

**Where the lost project actually lives.** It is learned late, and secondhand, and it stings for a week:

> "I found out in March, from her sister, that she signed with someone else in January."

**The Sunday version (the quoting session, not the bedroom):**

> "I'm booked till March and I have no idea where June comes from, and I keep telling everyone the market
> is slow because that's easier than looking at how many people I never got back to."

**The correction that makes the June line honest. [REV 2]** June is not a reply-speed problem on its own,
it is a sell-cycle problem. A project sold in March installs in November. June had to be sold in January.
So the true sentence, and the one a designer said she would save:

> **The reply you send today is not for June. It is for next January. That is why the hole is invisible
> until it is too late to fix.**

**Rules for using it.** Say it once, as an admission or as an overheard truth, never as an accusation.
Never follow it with "sound familiar?". Let it sit. The 2026-07-28 post proved the mechanism: the
admission with a number attached travels, the polished version does not.

## 7. THE OBJECTS, RANKED BY GUT DROP

Rank 1 is the hardest drop. Name the object, do not describe the feeling. The object does the work.
Tier A objects are designer-only and are the whole asset. Tier B objects are true but a photographer or a
wedding planner has them too, so **a Tier B object only ships with a design noun attached in the same
sentence.**

| # | Tier | Object | Why it drops |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | The Instagram DM from three days ago: **"hi! do you do full kitchens?"** Read. Not replied. | The exact shape of a project she can still see sitting there. Keep the lowercase hi. Keep the three days. |
| 2 | A | **"The other designer got back to us that night."** Said kindly. In an email. Or a review. | The loss with the reason attached. Nothing to argue with. |
| 3 | A | The missed call at **4:40pm from an unknown local number**, no voicemail. She was at the slab yard. | "The slab yard" is the most load-bearing noun in this file. Use it in the first two lines whenever possible. |
| 4 | A | The **contact form from Saturday morning**, opened Monday 9:12am. | She knows before she types the reply. |
| 5 | A | The **sister's text in March**: she signed with someone else in January. | Late, secondhand, unarguable. The real shape of the loss. |
| 6 | A | The **tote of memos in the back seat**, rubber-banded, unsorted since April. | Identity object. Proves insider standing. |
| 7 | A | The **fan deck in the door pocket** and the **High Point lanyard on the mirror**. | Identity objects. Warm the post, do not close it. |
| 8 | A | The **acknowledgment that says 22 weeks**, on a date she already promised out loud. | Adjacent reads this hardest of all. |
| 9 | B | The **Houzz Pro "you have a new lead" email**, the fourth this month, first three went cold. | She is paying for the thing that is not working. Safer than the Meta dashboard: more of them actually pay for it. |
| 10 | B | The **Notes app list titled "call back"**, nine first names, no phone numbers. | Her own handwriting, indicting her. Ship with a design noun: three of the nine are kitchens. |
| 11 | B | The **proposal tab still open from Thursday**, cursor blinking after "Dear". | Started, not sent. Ship with the scope line visible under it. |
| 12 | B | The **9:47pm client text: "quick question"** with a photo attached. | The job never actually stops. |
| 13 | B | The **unread badge at 11pm**, seven of them, none is the client she actually owes an answer to. | The badge never goes to zero. Weakest of the set, use last. |

**Rules.** Pain objects go at the top of a post. Identity objects (6 and 7) go in line two or three to

## 10. HOW TO USE THIS FILE

**Every agent writing to the interior niche runs four tests before a draft ships.**

**Test 1. Recognition.** Would a real studio owner read line one and say "holy shit, that is me"? Not
"useful", not "true", not "nice tip". Fail condition: an AI-automation founder, a roofer or a marketing
consultant could nod at it equally hard. Fix: add a noun from §2 or §7. Replace the category word with the
exact word. Not "supplies", say memos. Not "stone supplier", say the slab yard. Not "response time", say
"she answered that night".

**Test 2. The second reader (§1).** Can the remodeler, the cabinet shop or the kitchen-and-bath dealer
read the same post and think "that is my Tuesday too"? If only a solo residential designer can parse it,
it is half a post on LinkedIn. Fix it by adding one adjacent-legible line, not by removing the designer
detail.

**Test 3. Alignment.** Fail if the post could end with any service in the world attached to it.
Recognition without alignment is fortune-cookie content. Alignment without recognition is a brochure.
Breadth of recognition is not a failure by itself: the account's only breakout post was broad and it is
the best artifact we have.

**Test 4. The outsider test.** Would somebody outside this tribe understand every line? If yes, it is too
broad. Some jargon should be undecodable to an outsider. That is the signal, not the bug.

**The QA red-flag list (auto-fail, same tier as em dashes).**
- **British spelling anywhere.** enquiry, colour, favour, specced, personalised, organised, apologise,
  maths, grey. She stops reading at the u. This is the cheapest credibility tell in the file.
- Em dashes in published copy.
- Any claim that speed wins the job.
- Any consult-count hook.
- Any implied second client, plural client base, or "studios I work with".
- Any Instagram-DM build claim (see open item 1).
- Any unsourced stat: gender ratios, "worth five times what it was", third-party self-reported numbers.
- Banned vocabulary from §2.1 (leads, funnel, pipeline, speed-to-lead, response time, supplies).

**The three failure modes.**
1. **Writing to "small business owners" or "founders".** The instant slide back to the old ICP. A draft
   containing "business owners", "entrepreneurs", "founders" or "local businesses" without a design noun
   in the same three lines has failed. Rewrite or bin.
2. **Writing to other AI builders.** The most seductive failure, because it is the audience currently
   following the account. n8n node names, "agentic workflows", MCP, stack comparisons: P1 content for
   builders, not for her. Builders never buy. Never let them lead an interior post.
3. **Writing billboards.** "5 ways to respond to leads faster." "Why speed matters in 2026." True,
   universal, worthless. If a large generic brand could post the exact title, do not post it.

**Other standing rules.**
- **Register.** The 2026-07-28 evidence stands: honest first-person with a number attached broke out at
  1,500 impressions, 95 percent out of network. Framework and tool posts cap near 100 impressions,
  in-network. Run **1 story per 3 posts**, keep authority in the other two, and put a tribal noun from
  §2 or §7 into the first two lines of every one of them.
- **Write once, run three places.** Every LinkedIn post should be reusable near-verbatim as an Instagram
  caption and as a cold-email body. Same writing cost, three surfaces, and two of them actually reach the
  designer.
- **Ship.** No reach signal, comment count, or in-niche-commenter rule may block publishing. Findings
  append to the performance log. The number one measured leak is under-shipping.
- **Stance.** She is not the problem. She is on a jobsite. Attack the axis, never the designer.
- **Review trigger.** Rebuild this file if `current-strategy.md` changes the niche, if the first paying
  client is not in this tribe, or at the end of the 90-day window on 2026-10-22.
