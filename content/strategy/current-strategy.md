## PRICING FRAME (CEO ruling, 2026-08-10, adopts Decision C of `2026-08-09-dm-lane-decision-brief.md`)
**The number does not change. What it is called changes, and a condition is attached.**

$500 was being sold as a founding discount. Research says that is not how a US buyer reads it. A live n8n
job ad pays contract engineers **$500-$1,200 per build** for work the hiring agency resells at $4,000-$10,000,
and Fiverr sellers in Pakistan and Bangladesh price n8n packages at $80 / $650 / $1,100. At $500 Usama is
not offering a discount, he is quoting the wholesale subcontractor rate and entering a comparison set he
loses on review count, because he has zero reviews. A published competitor ladder starts its *pilot* at
$1,500. So $500 is not buying trust, it is buying position in the wrong comparison set and selecting for
the buyer who wanted cheap.

**The rule, effective now:**
1. **$500 is a proof purchase, not a discount.** Two founding slots, bought in exchange for something
   specific, not marked down.
2. **It never ships without the clause.** A written testimonial-and-reference commitment is part of the
   deal and is stated before payment: a named testimonial with the real numbers, and a reference call
   for one future prospect. That clause is the entire reason the price is $500. No clause, no $500.
3. **$1,500 is the minimum after the first 2 clients.** Not a target, a floor.
4. **Never say "discount", "cheap", "founding rate" or a struck-through price.** Say what it is: the
   first two builds are priced against proof because proof is what this business is short of.
5. **This is a sales-call and DM instrument, not content.** Do not announce it in the feed. An account
   with zero clients publishing a pricing change reads as an announcement nobody asked for.

Confidence on this call is medium, per the brief's own grading, and it is reversible: it changes framing
and adds a condition, it does not move the number. Revisit at the first delivered client.
