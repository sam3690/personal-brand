# Winning Post Patterns — AI Automation / Agency niche

> The swipe file. Synthesised from analysis of high-engagement LinkedIn posts by AI-automation &
> agency sellers (2026). "Winning" = **100+ engagements** (comments + reactions + reposts + saves).
> Agents 2/3/5 obey this. Sources at the bottom.

## The bar (what actually drives 100+ in THIS niche)
- **Comments > saves > reposts > reactions.** A 15+ word comment ≈ 15x the weight of a like.
  Comments drive ~70% of reach in the first 60–90 min. Design every post to earn a *real reply*.
- **Saves are the distribution multiplier** — lists, frameworks, "how I built it" breakdowns get saved.
- **Specificity is the moat.** Real numbers, named tools (n8n, GHL, Apollo, Claude), real client
  outcomes. 54% of LinkedIn posts are AI-generated and get ~45% less reach — generic = buried.
- **Lead with the result or the contrarian claim, not the wind-up.** First ~210 chars decide it.

## The 6 winning hook formulas (with AI-automation examples)
1. **Specific-number / result** — "I built an AI follow-up agent for one agency. It recovered 28 dead leads in month one. Here's the workflow:"
2. **Contrarian / unpopular opinion** — "Unpopular opinion: 90% of 'AI agents' agencies sell are just a chatbot with a markup."
3. **Pain-point confession** — "For 6 months I sold custom AI builds that broke in production. Then I changed one thing."
4. **Case study / client outcome first** — "A client was losing 3 deals a week to slow follow-up. One n8n workflow fixed it. Numbers below:"
5. **Unexpected comparison** — "Building a reliable AI agent is like plumbing. Nobody claps, but everything floods without it."
6. **Resource/list tease** — "7 n8n workflows that print money for agencies (I've shipped all 7):"

Hook rules: ≤210 chars before "see more" · 8–12 words per line · one idea per line · no wind-up,
no "I've been thinking", no bait ("comment YES"). The hook must be TRUE and paid off by the body.

## Format → engagement (pick deliberately)
| Format | Typical engagement | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 🏆 Carousel / document | ~20–24% (≈4x text) | "How I built X", frameworks, breakdowns, before/after |
| Story (vulnerable, specific) | ~8% | founder journey, a build that broke |

## Copy-paste body skeletons (the ones that win for AI-automation sellers)

**A. Case Study (result-first) — best for lead gen**
```
[Client outcome, quantified, in line 1]

[The before: their specific friction, in their world]
[The move you made, in operator terms — name the tools/nodes]
[One hard result number]
[The universal lesson for anyone reading]

[A real question that invites operators to share their version]
```
e.g. "A 2-person agency was losing 3 deals/week to slow replies. I built a 6-node n8n agent that
answers every WhatsApp lead in <60s and books the call. Month one: 28 recovered leads, 4 closed…"

**B. Data-backed contrarian — best for authority/debate**
```
Unpopular opinion: [bold claim challenging the AI-hype norm]

Most [agencies/founders] believe [the assumption].
After [N builds / a real benchmark], here's what's actually true:

→ [stat 1]
→ [stat 2]
→ [stat 3]

The real lever isn't [the hyped thing]. It's [your differentiated take].

[Question: "what are you seeing?"]
```

**C. Build-in-public breakdown (carousel) — best for saves + "how did you do that?" DMs**
```
Intro post: "I built [thing] that [result]. Here's the exact system 👇" + a real question.
Slides: 1 cover(result) · 1 the problem · 4–6 the build steps (name each node/tool) ·
        1 the bug that nearly killed it · 1 the result · 1 "save this / what would you automate?"
```

**D. 5-Lessons list — best for saves**
```
[N] things [N client builds / 500 workflows] taught me about [topic]:

1. [Lesson] — [one-line proof]
2 … 5 …

The one that cost me the most to learn: [#].

What would you add?
```

**E. Soft product showcase — best for inbound**
Lead with the PROBLEM and a real screenshot/workflow of the fix (not the product). Generates
"how did you do that?" comments → your warmest inbound. Consultative close, never a pitch.

## AI-automation-specific rules
- **Show the work.** A screenshot of the actual n8n canvas / the GHL pipeline / the result beats any claim.
- **Make the client the hero, you the operator.** Quantify everything; anonymise if needed.
- **End with a question an operator can answer from experience** ("what's the one workflow you'd never give up?"), not "thoughts?".
- **Niche to 2–3 topics** (AI sales automation, n8n builds, agency ops) — topical authority compounds reach.

## Don'ts (2026 penalties)
- No bait CTAs ("comment GUIDE") · no external links in body (-60% reach) · no pure cert/win celebration
  posts (trains the algo to show you to non-buyers) · no identical format daily · no generic AI cadence.

## Hook-Payoff Integrity (THE LAW — this is the rule the bad carousel broke)
The payoff must match the **shape** the hook promised. Top performers never break this. Justin Welsh's
whole method: "the body delivers on exactly what the trailer promised."

- **A number promises a count of DISTINCT things.** "5 boring automations" promises 5 separate, standalone
  automations. If the slides show one connected pipeline in 5 steps, it's broken: the reader was promised
  a *list of 5*, you delivered *one process*. Same trap: "7 workflows" that are really 7 steps of one workflow.
- **Match the shape:**
  | Hook promises | Payoff MUST be | NOT |
  |---|---|---|
  | "5 X" / "7 X" (a count) | that many discrete, independently-usable items | one thing in N steps |
  | "How I built X" (a process) | the ordered steps of that ONE build | a grab-bag of unrelated tips |
  | "3 mistakes" | 3 named, standalone mistakes | 3 stages of one story |
  | "Before/after" | the old way then the new way, both concrete | only the new way |
- **Carousel rule:** each counted item = its own standalone slide the reader could screenshot alone. If
  slide 4 only makes sense after slide 3, it's a process wearing a list's clothes. Rebuild the slides as a
  real list, or change the hook. Never let the rendered deck drift from the intro's promise.
- **Pre-publish test:** cover the hook, read only the body/slides, ask "what did this promise?" If that
  answer isn't the hook, one of them is wrong.

## Clarity / offer-legibility (a cold founder must "get it" in one read)
Operator-deep is good. Operator-*only* is a leak. A non-technical founder must finish the post knowing, in
plain words, what Usama does for them.
- Every post carries ONE plain-language line a non-engineer gets: what breaks, what the system does, what
  changes. e.g. "this is the thing that answers your leads in 60 seconds so you stop losing deals."
- Name the tools (n8n, Clay, Apollo) as PROOF, never as the point. The point is the outcome in the buyer's
  world: deals saved, hours back, no ghosted leads.
- If a line only lands for someone who already builds automations, add the buyer "so what."

## The Teach format (how-to / playbook) — a first-class P3 format, currently underused
The format Usama admires ("how to actually use Claude," "how to automate X"). Wins on SAVES + authority.
Nate Herk and Liam Ottley own this in the niche by teaching step-by-step, no skipped steps, safe for a
non-technical founder. Four proven teach structures (2026):
1. **Playbook** — "How to [outcome] in [N] steps." 3-5 concrete do-it-today steps, each names the exact
   move + tool, ends with the result if you run all N.
2. **Case Story** — challenge → the decision → the outcome → the one lesson. Best for P1 build stories.
3. **Mistakes List** — "[N] mistakes I made building [X]," each mistake one line + the fix. Best for saves.
4. **Before/After** — the manual old way vs the automated new way, both quantified.
Teach rule: the reader must be able to DO the thing (or clearly see how) from the post alone. Vague teach is
the worst of both worlds (no save, no authority). Give the real steps.

## Proven frameworks from named top performers (clone the skeleton, fill with Usama's real builds)
Don't invent a system. Clone one that already wins.
- **Justin Welsh — Trailer / Body / CTA.** Line 1 breaks the scroll, line 2 adds tension, last line before
  "see more" promises a payoff worth the click. Body delivers that exact payoff, scannable. CTA = one real
  question. His meta-move: turn every post that wins into a template and reuse it. (That's the point of this file.)
- **Nick Saraev — number + humbling outcome, failure-framed, named system.** Hook = a specific number tied to
  an unexpected/humbling result. Tell the "I messed this up" version, not the flex. Give your system a NAME
  so it's memorable and reusable (his is "DOE"). Contrarian takes on agency norms earn the comments.
- **Nate Herk / Liam Ottley — teach like you learned it.** Step-by-step, zero skipped steps, safe for a
  non-technical founder. Accessibility IS the moat in this niche.
