## Berman's 3 C's

A cold email structure: **Compliment** (something specific and real about them), **Case Study** (proof you've solved this exact problem for someone like them), **Call-to-Action** (one clear, low-friction ask).

**Audit against your current touch 1:** you already have the Case Study (the proof-line added this session: "recently built this exact setup for an interior-design studio...") and the CTA ("worth 15 minutes?"). You're missing a genuine **Compliment**. Generic flattery reads as spam; specific and true does not.

**Where to get the compliment, free:** the Apify Google Maps batch already captured `rating` and `reviews` for every local-service lead (`../../business/prospects/batch-04-2026-07-23-austin-local.csv`). "Noticed you're sitting at 4.9 stars across 200+ reviews, that's rare" is true, specific, and takes zero extra research.

## Aaron Ross's Cold Calling 2.0 lesson

Ross's actual innovation at Salesforce was splitting **prospecting** from **closing** into separate roles (SDR feeds, AE closes), because context-switching between "find strangers" and "build trust with someone warm" tanks performance at both.

**Applied to a solo operator:** you can't split the role, but you can split the **time**. Batch prospecting (list building, sending touch 1s) into one block of the day; keep replies, calls, and relationship-building in a separate block. Don't interleave them, the mental gear-shift between "cold blast" and "warm conversation" costs more than it looks like.

Ross's other core lesson: **referrals and inbound beat interruption.** Cold email is the volume floor, not the ceiling. Every warm reconnect (`../playbooks/warm-outreach-reconnect.md`) that ends in a referral is worth many cold sends, in Ross's own data referral-sourced deals close faster and bigger.

## Applied cadence
- Morning block: send new touch-1s + due follow-ups (cold, batched).
- Afternoon/evening: reply to anyone who engaged, warm reconnects, discovery calls (relationship mode).
- Never write a new cold email and reply to a warm lead back-to-back. Separate the modes.
