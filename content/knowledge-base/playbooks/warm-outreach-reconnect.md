# Warm Outreach & Reconnect Playbook

> The fastest path to a first client is almost never a cold stranger. It's someone who
> already knows you. Hormozi's Core Four ranks warm outreach as the highest-conversion
> lead source you own. You said your warm list is "a few, loosely" — this surfaces it
> and gives you the exact scripts. Voice rule: no em dashes.

---

## Why warm beats cold for client #1

- A cold prospect has to believe you exist, are real, and are competent, all in 60 seconds.
- A warm contact already grants you the first two. You only have to make the ask.
- Reply rates: cold email ~2-6%, warm reconnect ~30-50%. Ten warm messages can outperform 200 cold ones for landing the *first* deal.

---

## Step 1 — Surface your list (do this first, today)

You have more warm contacts than you think. List 10 names from these buckets. Don't overthink, just write names:

1. **Past clients / employers** — the 4 years of backend + healthcare + agency work. Anyone you built for.
2. **Ex-colleagues** — people who saw you ship and would vouch for you.
3. **People who engaged your content** — anyone who commented, DMed, or reacted to a LinkedIn/X post. They raised their hand.
4. **Cold prospects who replied at all** — even a "not now" is warm-ish. Reconnect later.
5. **Community peers** — n8n forum, AI/automation Discords, indie hacker circles.
6. **Warm intros** — anyone who could introduce you to an agency owner.

> Give me the 10 names + one line of context each, and I'll draft a personalized reconnect for every one. Without names I can't personalize, and generic warm outreach converts like cold.

---

## Step 2 — The A-C-A script (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask)

Hormozi's warm-outreach structure. Never open with a pitch. The goal of message 1 is a *reply*, not a sale.

**A — Acknowledge** the relationship/context. Real, specific.
**C — Compliment** something genuine and current about them.
**A — Ask** a low-friction question that opens a door. No pitch yet.

### Template 1 — past client / employer
> Hey {{name}}, it's been a while since {{shared context, e.g. "the intake system I built for you in 2024"}}. Saw {{specific recent thing they did}}, nice move. Quick question: are you (or anyone you rate) still doing manual lead follow-up, or have you automated it? Been going deep on that lately and wanted your read.

### Template 2 — someone who engaged your content
> Hey {{name}}, thanks for {{their comment/reaction on X post}} the other week. Genuine question, not a pitch: how does your team handle leads that come in after hours right now? Building something in that exact gap and want to pressure-test it against real setups.

### Template 3 — ex-colleague / peer (for referrals)
> Hey {{name}}, hope {{company/role}} is treating you well. I'm taking on 2 builds this month: AI systems that answer inbound leads in under 60 seconds and book them automatically. If anyone in your circle runs ads and hates slow follow-up, mind pointing them my way? Happy to return the favor.

---

## Step 3 — The transition to the ask (only if they engage)

When they reply, don't dump the offer. Mirror their pain first, then offer the Loom.

> That's the exact gap I close. I build an AI system that answers every lead in under 60 seconds, qualifies it, and books the call. Fixed $500, live in 7 days, you own the whole thing. And if it isn't live and answering in 7 days, you don't pay. Want a 2-min Loom of one running?

(Guarantee line = Tier 1 from `grand-slam-offer.md`.)

---

## Step 4 — The referral multiplier

Every warm contact, even ones who won't buy, gets one referral ask:

> Even if it's not for you, who's the one agency owner you know who'd care about this? A name is worth a lot to me right now, and I'll make you look good to them.

One good referral into a warm intro closes faster than 100 cold emails.

## Rules
- Message 1 never pitches. It earns a reply.
- Personalize every send. Generic warm = cold.
- Track sends in a simple list (name, sent date, replied Y/N, referral asked Y/N).
- 5-10 quality warm sends/day beats blasting. This is a scalpel, not the cold cannon.
